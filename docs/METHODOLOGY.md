# Methodology (analysis version `p1-methods-2026.11-s3`)

All vector data is stored in WGS 84. Areas and distances are computed in the project's local UTM zone (`EPSG:326xx/327xx`, chosen from the coordinates). Every metric records a **state** (`available`, `unknown`, `unavailable`, `invalid`, `blocked`, `not_applicable`) and an **output kind** (observed, reanalysis, calculated, classified, modelled, user-provided). Non-available values never carry a number. Every metric also records a **condition**: `existing` (it describes the land as it is today, so it is the same for every design) or `design` (it describes the proposed design). The condition comes from `urbanova/config/analyses.json` (see docs/ANALYSES.md); only design values are compared between designs. Every threshold, band and default has a basis record in `urbanova/config/basis.json` (standard, regulatory, literature, statistical, project choice or provisional), listed in each report.

## Climate & heat baseline (FR-17)
* Source: Open-Meteo Historical API, `models=era5`, daily Tmax/Tmin/Tmean, RH mean, 10 m max wind, precipitation, shortwave sum, `timezone=auto`. Units are checked against expected units; values outside physical ranges become missing. Fallback: NASA POWER daily (T2M_MAX/MIN, T2M, RH2M, PRECTOTCORR, ALLSKY_SFC_SW_DWN ×3.6 → MJ/m²); fill −999 → missing; wind excluded.
* Monthly climatology = mean of daily values per calendar month; hottest 3 months = highest mean Tmax.
* Indices per complete year (≥ 90 % days present), averaged: SU (Tmax > 25 °C), TR (Tmin > 20 °C) — ETCCDI; TX35, TX40 — provisional.
* P90/P95 = empirical percentiles of daily Tmax over the whole period.
* **TX90p (ETCCDI):** a second, cached ERA5 request fetches daily Tmax for the base period 1961–1990. For each calendar day the threshold is the 90th percentile (Hyndman–Fan type 8) of base Tmax in a 5-day window centred on that day; 29 February uses 28 February's threshold. TX90p = percentage of valid days in each complete analysis year with Tmax above its day's threshold, averaged over years. About 10 % means no change from the base climate. The analysis years lie outside the base, so no bootstrap is needed. If the NASA POWER fallback is in use (it starts in 1981) or the base request fails, TX90p is *unavailable* with the reason.

## Surrounding context (FR-15)
Overpass `way` features with `building`, `highway`, green `leisure/landuse/natural` tags and water, clipped to a 500 m buffer around the site. Shares are of the study-area area. Completeness warnings are always shown.

## Land cover (A2b, S7)
ESA WorldCover 10 m, newest map (2021, v200), from the Planetary Computer collection `esa-worldcover` (CC BY 4.0; attribution "ESA WorldCover project / Contains modified Copernicus Sentinel data processed by ESA WorldCover consortium"). The `map` COG is read onto a 10 m local-UTM grid (nearest, class codes kept; 0 = no data) over the context buffer (500 m, or the LST buffer when downscaling is on). Metrics: share of each class present (% of valid pixels) on the site and in the surrounding ring. The site-context statement names the dominant class of the ring and cites its metric. Classes and colours follow the product manual exactly (basis `land_cover.worldcover_classes`). Land cover describes today's land: it is site context only, listed beside H2, never a cause of a design hotspot. Limitations: a 2021 snapshot; about 77 % overall accuracy.

## Urban-form indicators (FR-16)
Features of each class are unioned (no double counting) and clipped to the site boundary. BCR = footprint ÷ area basis × 100 (area basis = boundary, or the verified document plot area if chosen). Green = green-space polygons + tree canopies with a stated radius. Hard surface = road + parking polygons. Open space = the union of designated open-space and green polygons, so an overlap (for example a tree canopy over a plaza) is counted once. Setback = minimum distance from a building edge to the boundary line. FAR = Σ(footprint × floors) ÷ area basis, only when floors are known for all buildings. Heights are never inferred.

## Land surface temperature (FR-18)
1. STAC search `landsat-c2-l2`, platforms landsat-8/9, scene cloud < 40 %, last 3 complete years; keep scenes in the hot months.
2. Read `qa_pixel` and `lwir11` windows; reproject to a 30 m local-UTM grid (nearest).
3. Mask QA bits 0–4 (fill, dilated cloud, cirrus, cloud, cloud shadow).
4. LST °C = DN × 0.00341802 + 149.0 − 273.15; drop values outside −30…80 °C.
5. Accept a scene if ≥ 60 % of site pixels are valid; composite = per-pixel median of ≤ 6 accepted scenes.
6. Metrics: site median & P90, ring median (site buffered by 60 m excluded, up to 1 km), site − ring difference (with per-scene min/max), share of site pixels ≥ study-area P90.
7. Classes from each pixel's mid-rank percentile within the study area (ties share one rank): < 25 cooler, 25–75 typical, 75–90 warmer, ≥ 90 hottest decile — relative to this study area only. A uniform field is therefore 'typical', never a hotspot.
8. If the site covers fewer than 9 native 100 m thermal pixels, the module is marked partial with a mixed-pixel warning.
9. **Site position among the surroundings (statistical):** the composite is aggregated to 90 m blocks (3 × 3 pixels, close to the 100 m native thermal resolution); only blocks fully inside the ring with all pixels valid are used. The site covers about n blocks (site pixels ÷ 9), so it is compared with averages of the same size: `lst_site_percentile_in_surroundings` = share of 999 seeded draws of n ring blocks whose mean is cooler than the site's mean (ties count half); `lst_site_minus_surroundings_z` = (site mean − ring block mean) ÷ (ring block standard deviation ÷ √n). Fewer than 10 blocks → *unknown*. Finding HEAT-LST-01 uses the percentile: ≥ 95 high, ≥ 90 moderate (the conventional one-sided 5 % and 10 % levels). It is reported only when the site − surroundings difference of medians is at least 1 K (about the uncertainty of Landsat surface temperature; a provisional project choice), so a statistically unusual but physically small difference is not flagged. Neighbouring blocks are correlated, so the percentile is a position, not an exact p-value.
10. **Hot cluster, site context (H2):** Getis–Ord Gi* on the 90 m block means of the whole study area (blocks with all pixels valid), binary distance band of 3 blocks (270 m) including the cell itself; one-sided conditional-permutation p-values (x_i fixed, 999 seeded permutations, p = (k + 1)/1000, the PySAL esda method, implemented in `analysis/spatial_stats.py`); Benjamini–Hochberg FDR at q = 0.05 across blocks; hot clusters only (z > 0). `lst_site_hot_cluster_share_pct` = share of the blocks touching the site that are in a significant cluster; the cluster outline is a map layer. It describes today's land: it is reported as site context, never as a design hotspot or a cause. Conditional permutation assumes no global spatial autocorrelation, so on smooth fields warm patches are reported as clusters (recorded in the tests; spec §3.3 note).
11. Where NDVI was also computed, mean NDVI of hottest-decile pixels vs the rest is reported as co-location only (not causation).
12. **Downscaling to 30 m (optional, S7; run setting `lst_downscale`, off by default):** the Landsat thermal band is 100 m, so the 30 m product is a resampled 100 m signal. When the setting is on, the run adds the vegetation and land-cover modules and `analysis/lst_downscale.py` sharpens the composite:
    * **Predictors** on the 30 m grid (area-weighted average): Sentinel-2 NDVI and NDBI = (SWIR − NIR)/(SWIR + NIR) from B11, both median composites over the LST study area; WorldCover class fractions for every class covering ≥ 1 % of the study area. Albedo (spec §3.4 item 1) is not used: no Sentinel-2 broadband-albedo coefficients have been verified for this project.
    * **Training:** 90 m blocks (3 × 3 cells) with every cell valid; random forest with 200 trees, minimum 3 blocks per leaf, seed 0 [R-30] (basis `lst_downscale.*`).
    * **Prediction and residual correction:** predict every 30 m cell; add each block's residual (block LST − mean of its 30 m predictions), so the field averages back to the 90 m LST exactly (TsHARP-style [R-31]).
    * **Checks on every run:** coarse consistency (maximum aggregation error; the field is used only if it is < 0.1 °C); leave-one-scene-out RMSE (train on the median of the other scenes, predict the held-out scene's 90 m blocks out-of-bag — each block only by trees that did not see it — with each scene's mean removed because the model predicts the pattern and the level changes with the day's weather); scale check (train at 270 m, predict 90 m with residual correction, RMSE against the 90 m LST), compared with the published urban range of about 1–3.5 °C [R-32] — above the range adds a caution warning.
    * **Skipped** with a reason when fewer than 2 scenes, fewer than 30 complete 90 m blocks, or no vegetation composite is available. The 100 m results always stand.
    * **Outputs:** `lst_ds_site_median_c`, `lst_ds_site_p90_c` (output kind *modelled*, condition *existing*, validation figures in `quality`), and the layer "LST downscaled to 30 m (ML-downscaled, modelled; leave-one-scene-out RMSE … °C)". Never used for design hotspots, causes or scenario differences.

**Access:** this module calls the Planetary Computer mirror only after that route is approved in `urbanova/config/providers.json`; otherwise it reports *blocked*.

## Vegetation (FR-20)
Blocked until the Planetary Computer access route is approved (same as LST).
Sentinel-2 L2A (`sentinel-2-l2a`) B04, B08 (10 m) and SCL (20 m → 10 m nearest), last 365 days, one scene per date, ≤ 4 scenes. Reflectance = (DN − 1000)/10000 for processing baseline ≥ 04.00, else DN/10000. SCL classes 0, 1, 3, 8, 9, 10, 11 masked. NDVI = (NIR − Red)/(NIR + Red), median composite. Vegetated share = NDVI ≥ 0.2. Planned green polygons are compared with observed NDVI where present. With LST downscaling on (S7), B11 (SWIR, 20 m → 10 m nearest) is read too, an NDBI median composite is formed with the same masks, and both composites cover the LST study area (`lst_buffer_m`) instead of `ndvi_buffer_m`.

## Solar position and shade (FR-19)
NOAA/Spencer solar declination; elevation and azimuth at true solar time (no time-zone assumption). Building shadow = union of footprint, translated footprint (offset = h / tan(elevation) away from the sun) and the swept quadrilateral of every edge — exact for vertical prisms on flat ground. Shade structures and trees with height + canopy radius cast translated canopies. Hourly 07:00–17:00; shade duration = Σ hourly shaded share × 1 h.

## Hot-day weather (A1, for heat stress)
A third cached ERA5 request (Open-Meteo archive, hourly, UTC) fetches air temperature, relative humidity, 10 m wind and the *instantaneous* direct-normal radiation for the analysis period (SolarCal derives diffuse and reflected radiation from the direct beam). Hot days = days in the three hottest months with Tmax ≥ the period's 90th percentile of daily Tmax (basis `climate.hot_day_definition`, project choice). UTC is converted to true solar time (UTC + longitude/15 h + equation of time, Spencer series) and each variable is linearly interpolated to the solar hours 07–17, then averaged over the hot days. Not available with the NASA POWER fallback (no hourly ERA5); heat stress is then blocked with that reason.

## Heat stress in sun and shade (A9)
For each solar hour of the hot day: mean radiant temperature in sun = air temperature + ΔMRT from SolarCal (ASHRAE 55 Appendix C, `pythermalcomfort.solar_gain` 4.6.2: standing, short-wave absorptivity 0.7, sky-view fraction 1, ground reflectance 0.2, averaged over body orientations 0–180° in 15° steps); in shade the same with no direct beam on the body. UTCI = `pythermalcomfort.utci(Ta, Tmrt, v10 (≥ 0.5 m/s), RH)` with its input limits on (out-of-range hours are reported, never extrapolated). Categories (Bröde et al. 2012): 26–32 moderate, 32–38 strong, 38–46 very strong, > 46 extreme heat stress. Metrics: peak UTCI in sun and shade, hours with UTCI in sun ≥ 38 and ≥ 32 (existing condition: the same for every design), and the design metrics *sunlit hard surface / open space during very strong heat stress* = Σ over those hours of the area outside that hour's exact shadow polygons (m²·h). Surfaces are assumed at air temperature (long-wave radiation from hot paving is not modelled, so heat stress over paving is underestimated); wind is the regional ERA5 10 m wind.

## Design hotspots (A8)
* **H1 sun-exposed hard surface:** hard surface = road, parking and open space (assumed paved unless its `surface` says grass, soil, planted, …), clipped to the site, minus buildings. It is split exactly by every hour's shadow polygons; each piece counts its hours in sun at UTCI ≥ 38 (n_vs) and ≥ 32 (n_s). Intensity: high if n_vs ≥ 4; moderate if n_s ≥ 4 or n_vs ≥ 1; low otherwise. High and moderate pieces are unioned into connected polygons (slivers < 1 m² dropped).
* **H3 shade deficit:** open and green space farther than D = 30 m from any tree canopy or shade structure and in sun at 15:00, produced only when UTCI in sun at 15:00 ≥ 38; intensity as H1.
* **H4 planning violation:** from failed verified-rule checks — building parts inside the setback band, buildings above a height or floor limit (polygons); coverage, green, open-space and FAR shortfalls are site-wide (area = shortfall in m², no polygon). Intensity by shortfall relative to the limit: ≥ 25 % high, 10–25 % moderate, < 10 % low.
* **Merging:** located hotspots overlapping by more than half of the smaller one are merged (types kept, higher intensity), repeated until stable; site-wide hotspots are not merged.
* **Size, severity, priority:** small < 200 m² or < 1 % of the site; large ≥ 1,000 m² or ≥ 5 %; otherwise medium. Severity from the §4.2 intensity × size table; any severity resting on an unapproved project-choice value (size classes, intensity hours, D, H4 bands, hot-day definition, SolarCal sky view and reflectance) is labelled and capped at moderate. Numbered by severity, then area.
* **Supporting measurements and causes:** per hotspot, area-weighted sun-hours, elevation-weighted sun-hours, hours at strong / very strong heat stress, UTCI in sun vs shade, hard-surface share and composition, distance to the nearest canopy, each with the ids of the design metrics it derives from. Causes come only from the fixed rules LARGE_PAVED_AREA (≥ 70 % hard), NO_AFTERNOON_SHADE (≥ 80 % in sun at 15:00), NO_TREES_NEARBY (nearest canopy > D), EXPOSED_WEST_EDGE (sunlit strip within 5 m west of a façade at 15:00), ORIENTATION_EXPOSES_STREETS (street axis within 20° of east–west and height-to-width < 0.5), EXCEEDS_RULE (H4), each with its evidence and metric ids.
* Each design hotspot gives one finding (classification *modelled outcome*) with its recommendations targeted at the hotspot. A failed verified rule stays a *high* planning-check finding and links to its H4 hotspot. SHADE-01 (team-chosen bands for open-space sun exposure) was removed in favour of H1/H3.

## Intervention options and the optimiser (A10)
* **Catalogue** (`config/interventions.json`): I1 trees (grid spacing 6 / 9 / 12 m, canopy radius 3 m, height 6 m, trunks ≥ 2 m from façades), I2 shade structures (50 / 100 % of the hotspot, 3.5 m), I3 paving converted to planting (25 / 50 %), I4 cool paving (area treated only), I5 parking reduced (25 / 50 %; lost spaces at 25 m² per space), I6 fix a planning violation (trim building parts out of the setback band; convert the largest paved areas to green until a green / open-space minimum is met), I7 orientation check (no generated layout). Sizes are project choices awaiting approval (basis `interventions.*`).
* **Which hotspots:** the 5 highest-priority design hotspots (basis `interventions.max_hotspots`); an intervention applies when it lists the hotspot's type and one of its causes.
* **Measured effect (partial re-run):** the option is applied to a copy of the design and only the design analyses are re-run (urban form, shade, heat stress; urban form and planning checks for H4), reusing the run's site data (hot day, hottest months); building shadows and the hot-day UTCI table are reused when unchanged, so results equal a full re-run. Benefit units: H1 = Σ over very-strong UTCI hours of the sunlit hard surface inside the hotspot (m²·h); H3 = open space in the hotspot in sun at 15:00 and farther than D from shade (m²); H4 = shortfall against the verified rule (m²). Only options with a positive measured effect are kept. Each option also reports the hotspot's area-weighted hours in sun at very strong heat stress before → after.
* **Ranking:** with every option priced in one currency, the Pareto front is taken on benefit and typical cost, with roles best value (benefit per cost), largest benefit and cheapest effective. Without common prices, options are compared only within each intervention on benefit and quantity (best value = benefit per unit of quantity); largest benefit is hotspot-wide. Cool paving is listed separately and never ranked against shade.
* **Package:** with `budget_amount` and `budget_currency`, the best-value priced option of each hotspot is added greedily (highest benefit per cost first) while the typical total stays within the budget; the combined design is re-evaluated together and kept as a draft. It can be saved as a *draft* scenario, which cannot be analysed until approved.
* **Recommendations:** for design hotspots, only from measured options (one per intervention), with the measured effect, cost range or "price required", two separate confidences (effect: high when measured in the hotspot's own unit with reviewed inputs, medium with flagged features or missing heights, low for I4; cost: high for a project rate under 2 years old, medium for a library quotation or approved list, low for an old or missing price), trade-offs including irrigation water ("value required" until a sourced value is recorded) and maintenance, required inputs and the evaluation method. Literature effects are context only, never attached to the site.

## AI-written insights and answers (S4)
* **Opt-in:** per project (Project settings → "Allow AI-written insights and suggestions"), off by default. Off, or with no provider available, the deterministic summary is used.
* **Evidence pack** (`urbanova/ai/evidence.py`): module statuses, ranked design hotspots (causes, values with metric ids), site context, findings, up to 40 metrics plus every metric a hotspot cites, the measured best options, every module limitation (ids `lim_n`) and the selected questions with their deterministic answerability. Never files, drawings, geometry, personal data or keys. The priority order is computed, not written by the model.
* **Model call** (`urbanova/ai/llm.py`): OpenAI-compatible chat API, JSON output, providers in the order of `URBANOVA_LLM_PROVIDERS` (Groq, OpenRouter, a local Ollama), keys only in the server environment. A provider that fails, times out or returns 429 is skipped for the rest of the UTC day; `URBANOVA_LLM_MAX_CALLS_PER_DAY` caps calls; offline mode disables it.
* **Validator** (`urbanova/ai/validate.py`): rejects invalid JSON or shape, unknown claim types or ids, any number not in the cited evidence (after normalising number words, k, %, m² / ha / km², °C, with rounding tolerance), cause codes the hotspot lacks, hotspots mentioned without being cited, temperature predictions or literature numbers for the site, simulation or CFD claims, a changed priority order, statutory-approval, guaranteed-savings or health claims, and any limitation not disclosed.
* **Flow:** cache (evidence-pack hash, model, prompt version) → model → validator → one retry with the validator's messages → next provider on a provider failure → deterministic fallback (`urbanova/ai/fallback.py`, same structure, passes the same validator), labelled "AI text unavailable". Every stored insight records generated_by, provider, model, prompt version and attempts.
* **Ask:** with AI on, a free-text question gets a validated AI answer, else the deterministic answer; questions asking for a temperature prediction always get the fixed refusal.
* **Data rule (8 Nov 2026):** real (non-synthetic) projects are sent only to a local model; cloud providers serve synthetic or test projects unless the operator sets `URBANOVA_AI_CLOUD_FOR_REAL_DATA=1`.
* **Chat (S8, `urbanova/ai/chat.py`):** the Results tab's question box is a conversation about the open run, stored per run (`chat_messages`). The model may ask for read-only tools with a JSON request (`{"call": {"tool", "args"}}`): `list_hotspots`, `get_hotspot` (record, causes, measured options with cost), `get_metric` (value, unit, condition, method) and `compare` (the latest comparison that includes the run). At most 4 tool calls per question. Tool results are added to the evidence for that question only; the final `{"answer": [claims]}` passes the same validator, with one retry, then the deterministic answer. Prediction and change requests ("delete", "approve", "apply" …) get a fixed answer without a model call. The last 6 turns are sent as text for follow-ups; earlier answers are never evidence. A tool loop or a validation failure does not spend a second provider. Evaluation: `scripts/eval_chat.py` → `docs/CHAT_EVALUATION.md`.
* **Suggestions (candidates only):** planning rules from a document's page text, kept only when the quote is found verbatim on the stated page and contains the value (stored as candidate rules, origin `ai_suggested`); feature types for unrecognised CAD layer names, restricted to the known types and stored as review items on the features, never applied.

## Planning checks (FR-16, FR-44/45)
Only user-verified rules. Ratio checks (coverage, green, open space, FAR) are *not assessed* while plot areas conflict in the document, or when the verified plot area differs from the boundary area by > 5 % and the user has not chosen the basis. Results are labelled "passes the configured geometric check", never "approved".

## Findings and recommendations (FR-22/23)
Configured in `urbanova/config/rules.json` with metric, severity bands, status (`standard` / `statistical` / `provisional_not_approved`, consistent with the basis record in `basis.json`; provisional findings are labelled "PROVISIONAL THRESHOLD — NOT APPROVED" in the UI and reports), justification and limitation. A provisional threshold never produces "high": the severity is capped at "moderate" while the threshold is unapproved, and the band it falls in is shown with it. Each finding states its affected area; climate findings describe the ~25 km reanalysis grid cell and are not drawn on the site. A verified-rule failure is rated high. Findings from existing-land analyses (climate, context, vegetation, surface temperature) are site context: they carry no design recommendations (decision S-8); recommendations come from design analyses. Modules that are blocked/unavailable/failed produce data-quality findings. Recommendation templates (`urbanova/config/recommendations.json`) state only the expected direction of impact.

## Comparison (FR-26/27)
Requires the latest successful run of each scenario, identical comparable settings and analysis version, and no design change since the run. Only design-dependent metrics are compared; units and method text must match. Optional scoring: min–max normalisation across compared scenarios using the user's weights; missing criteria are excluded (never 0); no ranking without weights.

**Trade-offs and the choice (S5).** Criteria a user may weight: heat stress (sun-exposed hard surface and open space during very strong UTCI, m²·h, lower is better), green-space share and shade hours on open space (higher is better), indicative cost (typical total in one currency; the baseline is 0; lower is better) and failed verified planning rules (fewer is better). Water joins when a sourced litres/day value exists. There are no default weights.
* **Trade-off chart:** each design at (typical cost, heat-stress m²·h); the Pareto front joins the designs not beaten on both by another. A design without a complete price or heat-stress value is listed, never plotted at zero.
* **What each alternative gives up:** parking spaces (parking area removed ÷ 25 m²), building footprint and floor area, trees and planting added with irrigation water (litres/day "value required" until sourced), and maintenance per year (from rates with a maintenance column, else "value required").
* **No weights:** no winner; the trade-offs and per-criterion results are shown with a sentence that the choice depends on the project's priorities.
* **With weights:** min–max normalised weighted mean (missing values excluded, never scored 0). The best is the highest-scoring design that fails no verified rule and, with a budget, has a complete price within it; excluded designs say why. Labelled "best under your priorities", with the weights used.
* **Robustness:** the choice is recomputed with each weight at ±20 %, one at a time: "robust" or "sensitive to …". Costs come from single-value rates, so the low/high cost re-test applies only when ranges exist (stated).

## Costs (FR-28)
**Option costs (S3):** quantity × low / typical / high unit cost with maintenance per year shown apart. Source priority: the project's own rates (one value, so low = typical = high; an optional `maintenance_per_year` column), then a quotation in `config/cost_library.json`, then a supervisor-approved list in it; otherwise "price required". The library starts empty (no credible public Egyptian unit costs were found); demo prices use the TEST rate table in test currency XTS. Prices older than 2 years are flagged; no inflation adjustment.

Quantities = differences in site-clipped totals between alternative and baseline (green added, hard surface removed, trees added, shade area added, footprint removed). A line is priced only when a user rate with matching unit exists; currencies are never summed together; stale (> 2 years) rates are flagged.
