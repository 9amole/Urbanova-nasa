# 2-minute demo script — URBANOVA Trend Detective (Space Apps 2026)

**0:00 — The problem (10 s).** "NASA measures hundreds of things about the Earth. But which of them are changing where *you*
live, by how much, and is the change real or just noise? Our Trend Detective answers that in under a minute."

**0:10 — A watched demo: New Cairo (30 s).**
1. Click **New Cairo, Egypt** under *Watch a demo*.
2. Narrate the captions as they appear: the question (heat), the place type (city), the pin on the map ("one NASA
   cell, about 55 km"), and the suggested variables with their simple definitions.
3. The result appears: "**+1.5 °C since 1981**: +0.33 °C per decade, 95 % range +0.16 to +0.52. Both tests agree it is
   significant."

**0:40 — Reading the chart, and "where" (20 s).** "The pale line is each month with the normal seasonal cycle removed,
because summer is always hotter than winter. The dark line is the yearly average, and the tests run on it. Green is the
trend, the shaded band its 95 % range." Click **Where is it changing?**: "all 80 NASA cells around Cairo are warming
significantly."

**1:00 — Opposite trends, and "not yet" (25 s).**
1. Click **Same planet, opposite rain**: "the Sahel is getting **wetter**, south-west Australia **drier**, both
   significant. The same climate change moves rain belts." Open *Where is it changing?* near Perth: the coast dries and a
   strip inland gets wetter.
2. Click **Sydney**: "**Not significant yet**: a drying hint, p = 0.07. The range includes zero, so we need a longer
   record."

**1:25 — Your own question (20 s).** Choose *Heat and temperature*, then *Farmland or river delta*, then click the Nile
Delta, then **Find variables**, then **Test for a trend** on *Night-time low temperature*: "**+0.42 °C per decade**
(+0.33 to +0.51), faster than the average temperature (+0.29): the nights are warming fastest." Change the period to
2000–2025 (+0.47 °C per decade, still significant), then **Copy link** to share it.

**1:45 — Part two (15 s).** "Signed in, *Zoom into a plot here* turns the regional trend into URBANOVA's site analysis:
surface temperature from Landsat, vegetation from Sentinel-2, and how hot it feels in sun and shade, so a regional trend
becomes design advice for a real street."

**2:00 — Close.** "Pick a question, a place, NASA data and two statistical tests. Anyone can be an Earth system trend
detective."
