# 2-minute demo script — URBANOVA Trend Detective (Space Apps 2026)

**0:00 — The problem (10 s).** "NASA measures hundreds of things about the Earth. But which of them are changing where *you*
live, by how much, and is the change real or just noise? Our Trend Detective answers that in under a minute."

**0:10 — A watched demo: New Cairo (30 s).**
1. Click **New Cairo, Egypt** under *Watch a demo*.
2. Narrate the captions as they appear: the question (heat), the place type (city), the pin on the map ("one NASA
   cell, about 55 km"), and the suggested variables with their simple definitions.
3. The result appears: "**+0.31 °C per decade**, 95 % confidence interval 0.17 to 0.45. Both tests agree it is
   significant. About 1.4 °C hotter afternoons since 1981."

**0:40 — Reading the chart (20 s).** "Each thin line is a month, with the normal seasonal cycle removed, because
summer is always hotter than winter. The dots are yearly averages. The green line is the trend, and the shaded band is
its 95 % confidence band. Green means significant."

**1:00 — Two tests, and what "not yet" means (25 s).**
1. Click **Sydney**: "**Not significant yet**: drying by about 0.1 mm a day per decade, p = 0.08. The interval includes
   zero, so we'd need a longer record."
2. Click **Sahel**: "Here the two tests disagree. The straight-line t-test says significant, but the Mann-Kendall test
   doesn't, because most months have no rain at all. A real detective checks with two methods."

**1:25 — Your own question (20 s).** Choose *Heat and temperature*, then *Farmland or river delta*, then click the Nile
Delta, then **Find variables**, then **Test for a trend** on *Night-time low temperature*: "**+0.43 °C per decade**
(0.37–0.50), faster than the average temperature (+0.31): the nights are warming fastest." Change the period to 2000–2025
(+0.49 °C per decade, still significant), then **Download CSV**.

**1:45 — Part two (15 s).** "Signed in, *Zoom into a plot here* turns the regional trend into URBANOVA's site analysis:
surface temperature from Landsat, vegetation from Sentinel-2, and how hot it feels in sun and shade, so a regional trend
becomes design advice for a real street."

**2:00 — Close.** "Pick a question, a place, NASA data and two statistical tests. Anyone can be an Earth system trend
detective."
